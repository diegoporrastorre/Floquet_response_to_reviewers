# Response to Referee 1 - Novelty

## Referee 1 Comment: 

“The manuscript “Floquet Topological Frequency-Converting Amplifier” proposes a Floquet-modulated singlemode bosonic system in which both the mode frequency and the decay rate are periodically driven. In Floquet-Sambe space, this leads to an effective non-Hermitian synthetic lattice, which the authors argue supports topological frequency conversion and directional amplification. The manuscript looks solid, and the proposed implementation in superconducting circuits is potentially interesting. However, in its presentform I find that the main physical message of the manuscript is not sufficiently clear.\
My main concern is that the paper reads as somewhat incremental with respect to the existing literature on non-Hermitian topological amplification, Floquet synthetic dimensions, and singular-value topology of input-output response matrices. I do not doubt that the calculations may be correct, but the manuscript does not yet make sufficiently transparent what the central conceptual advance is. Is the main result the minimality of the setup, namely that a single driven oscillator can realize this physics? Is the novelty the useof simultaneous frequency and dissipation modulation? Or is there a genuinely new topological amplification mechanism? At present this point is somewhat hidden behind the formal development, and the authors should state it much more explicitly.”\

### (iii) HN in synthetic frequency dimensions\

#### (iii-1) The part most related to our paper. Includes papers on HN models with multi-mode resonators (already discussed in the overleaf) + Fleury 2018 PRL paper.: Non-reciprocal gain in non-Hermitian time-Floquet systems, T.T. Koutserimpas and Romain Fleury, PRL 2018\

This paper was already cited in the first version. It is actually closely related: a simultaneous coupling of both hermitian and dissipative couplings between both oscillators leads to a “HN” dynamics in which light enters at \omega\_1 and leaves at \omega\_2, with some degree of amplification. The basic non-Hermitian HN dynamics is the same, only that there is no topological invariant/topological amplification, no topological physics and, more importantly, no incoherent pump that drives the non-Hermitian point-gap topology.\
In essence, their setup gives a\_1(\omega\_1) -> a\_2(\omega + \Omega), non-reciprocal up-conversion, but further sidebands are detuned by the effective electric field. It is a short of 2-level simplification of our approach (therefore, no topology, edge-states, etc…)\


### (iii-2) Concerning Hatano-Nelson models with multimode-resonators, this is the answer:

A second line of research concerns the realization of non-Hermitian Hatano–Nelsonmodels in synthetic frequency dimensions using dynamically modulated multimode resonators. In these systems, the cavity-mode index acts as a synthetic lattice coordinate, with modulation-induced couplings between modes giving rise to an effective tight-binding model in frequency space. This approach has enabled theoretical proposals for non-Hermitian skin effects \[Song et al., 2020], experimental demonstrations of non-Hermitian topological winding \[Wang et al., 2021], and studies of directional frequencyconversion associated with non-Hermitian topology \[Ye et al., 2025; Pontula et al., 2025]. In particular, electro-optic amplitude modulators can engineer dissipative hopping by periodically modulating the intracavity field transmission, providing a mechanism analogous to the decay-rate modulation employed in our scheme. These multimode implementations have primarily focused on classical optical dynamics, without explicitly addressing the quantum noise associated with topological frequency-converting amplification.\
There are, however, several important differences between these approaches and our proposal:\
(i) Minimal physical architecture. Our scheme relies on a single quantum harmonic oscillator rather than multiple physical cavity modes. The synthetic frequency dimension emerges from the Floquet–Sambe representation of a single bosonic degree of freedom, rather than from the mode index of a multimode resonator. This considerably reduces the number of physical degrees of freedom required to realize non-Hermitian topological physics and provides a minimal building block for topological frequency conversion and amplification. Furthermore, the proposed architecture is compatible with quantum platformssuch as superconducting microwave resonators, where individual bosonic modes and their dissipative couplings can be engineered and controlled.\
(ii) Distinctive Floquet phenomenology. The Floquet–Sambe construction naturally introduces an effective linear potential of the form (−nΩ) along the synthetic frequency dimension, analogous to a uniform electric field \[Martin, Refael, Halperin, 2017]. This feature, absent from the idealized translationally invariant synthetic lattices of multimode resonators, gives rise to a spatially varying topological structure. In particular, it determines the formation of topological domains and the emergence of Jackiw–Rebbi-likesingular modes, thereby selecting the frequency regions in which directional amplification occurs.\
(iii) Topological response amplification and quantum noise. Beyond non-Hermitian spectral topology and directional frequency transport, our work establishes a direct connection between local topological winding and enhanced frequency-resolved input-output response. Specifically, quasi-zero singular modes of the Floquet dynamical matrix give rise to directional response amplification and frequency conversion between selected sidebands. Moreover, our single-oscillator formulation allows for a complete quantum input-output treatment, including the noise accompanying the amplified response. This extendsthe predominantly classical description of synthetic-frequency Hatano–Nelson systems by characterizing the quantum fluctuations associated with topological frequency-converting amplification.\
In summary, our proposal combines a minimal single-oscillator realization of non-Hermitian topologywith a distinctive Floquet-induced frequency structure and a quantum description of directional amplification. The effective frequency gradient determines the topological domains and the preferred amplification frequencies, while the corresponding singular modes govern both the enhanced response and its associated noise. This establishes a route toward investigating topological frequency conversion and amplification at the level of individual quantum bosonic modes.**
