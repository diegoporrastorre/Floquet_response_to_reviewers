# Response to Referee 1 — Novelty and relationship to three research areas

We thank the referee for this comment. We agree that the manuscript should state its central conceptual advance more explicitly and distinguish it more carefully from existing work. **Our main contribution is the realization of directional topological amplification and frequency conversion in the Floquet sidebands of a single effective quantum harmonic oscillator, together with a description of how the Floquet frequency gradient determines the amplification channels and their quantum noise.** This contribution builds on established non-Hermitian topology and singular-value input-output theory. To clarify its relationship to previous work, we organize the relevant literature into three main research areas.

**(i) Floquet topology and topological frequency conversion.**

A first research direction uses periodic or quasiperiodic driving to engineer topological phases in synthetic frequency dimensions. A particularly relevant inspiration is the work of Martin, Refael, and Halperin, where two incommensurate drives generate a two-dimensional Floquet lattice with a nonzero Chern number, producing quantized energy transfer between the driving sources. Their construction also establishes the interpretation of the drive frequencies as an effective electric field in Floquet space. [Martin, Refael, and Halperin, *Phys. Rev. X* **7**, 041008 (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.02143).

Our work addresses a different physical observable and topological setting. We consider a bosonic oscillator whose frequency and decay rate are modulated at a common frequency, generating a one-dimensional, non-Hermitian Floquet–Sambe lattice. The observable is the directional conversion and amplification of an injected signal between Floquet sidebands. Its topological characterization involves a local point-gap winding and the singular modes of the response matrix, rather than a Chern number governing energy pumping between two incommensurate drives. Dissipation engineering and incoherent pumping therefore play an essential role in the amplification physics studied here.

Furthermore, the effective frequency gradient is an active ingredient in our results. It produces a frequency-dependent local topological structure and interfaces between topologically distinct regions. The associated Jackiw–Rebbi-like singular modes determine the frequency regions where signals are most efficiently injected and amplified. Thus, although the Floquet-lattice construction and its effective electric field have clear precedents, our work identifies their consequences for non-Hermitian response amplification.

**(ii) Floquet Liouvillians and periodically driven open quantum systems.**

A second research direction studies the interplay between periodic driving and dissipative quantum dynamics. This includes extensions of Floquet theory to Lindblad equations, the existence of effective time-independent Floquet Lindbladians, and systematic high-frequency expansions, as investigated by Dai, Shi, and Yi and by Schnell and collaborators. These works establish the theoretical framework for describing open Floquet systems; their central questions concern effective generators and dissipative evolution. [Dai *et al.*, *Phys. Rev. A* **93**, 032121 (2016)](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.93.032121); [Schnell *et al.*, *Phys. Rev. B* **101**, 100301(R) (2020)](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.101.100301); [Schnell *et al.*, *Phys. Rev. B* **104**, 165414 (2021)](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.104.165414).

Related applications include finite-time quantum heat engines, dissipative Floquet parity-time transitions, exceptional contours in driven Lindblad dynamics, and the preparation of nonequilibrium steady states in superconducting qubits. These studies demonstrate that periodic coherent control and dissipation already provide a rich setting for Floquet engineering. Our novelty claim therefore does not rest on combining periodic driving and dissipation alone. [Scopa *et al.* (2018)](https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.97.062121); [Li *et al.* (2019)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-08596-1); [Gunderson *et al.* (2021)](https://scholarworks.indianapolis.iu.edu//bitstreams/052bda80-ccc9-4135-8caa-a675a32d6994/download); [Chen *et al.* (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.134.090402).

An especially relevant comparison is the work of Koch, Hu, and Budich on Liouvillian topology and nonreciprocal dynamics in open Floquet chains. That work explicitly establishes topological dissipative transport, including effects of quantum jumps beyond an effective non-Hermitian Hamiltonian description. Its setting is a spatially extended fermionic chain, and its focus is the topology of the Floquet-Liouvillian propagator and the resulting particle transport. Our study instead concerns the frequency-resolved bosonic input-output response of one effective oscillator: the relevant synthetic coordinate labels Floquet harmonics, and the central observables are signal gain, frequency conversion, and noise. [Koch, Hu, and Budich, *Phys. Rev. Research* **8**, 023270 (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16742).

**(iii) Non-Hermitian transport and amplification in synthetic frequency dimensions.**

The closest research direction concerns non-Hermitian frequency lattices and nonreciprocal frequency conversion. Multimode resonators have enabled proposals for synthetic-dimensional skin effects, experimental measurements of non-Hermitian band winding, and directional frequency transport. Important examples include Song *et al.*, Wang *et al.*, Ye *et al.*, and Pontula *et al.* [Song *et al.* (2020)](https://ufolab.phys.hust.edu.cn/PhysRevApplied.14.pdf); [Wang *et al.* (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abf6568); [Ye *et al.* (2025)](https://olab.physics.sjtu.edu.cn/papers/2025/01.%20Rui%20Ye_light_2025.pdf); [Pontula *et al.* (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14299).

We acknowledge the substantial conceptual overlap with this literature. In particular, simultaneous phase and amplitude modulation already provides coherent and dissipative synthetic couplings in multimode resonators. The distinction is that those frequency lattices use distinct physical resonator modes as lattice sites, whereas our lattice consists of Floquet harmonics of a single effective bosonic degree of freedom. Here, “single oscillator” refers to the effective dynamical model; the proposed circuit implementation uses rapidly damped auxiliary modes to engineer its reservoirs.

Beyond this reduction in dynamical degrees of freedom, our work retains the intrinsic Floquet potential \(-n\Omega\). Compared with an ideal translation-invariant Hatano–Nelson frequency lattice, this gradient creates topological domains within frequency space. We connect their local winding to quasi-zero singular modes and to the resulting frequency-selective amplification. Our quantum input-output treatment further characterizes the noise accompanying this amplified response.

Koutserimpas and Fleury provide another particularly close precedent: their periodically modulated complex coupling between resonators produces nonreciprocal frequency conversion and gain. We therefore do not claim that Floquet nonreciprocal amplification itself is new. The additional result here is its realization and topological characterization in a single effective oscillator, where frequency-space domains and their singular modes organize the amplification channels. [Koutserimpas and Fleury, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **120**, 087401 (2018)](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.087401).

To answer the referee’s question directly, the conceptual advance combines **a minimal effective architecture, a distinctive role for the Floquet frequency gradient, and a quantum description of the resulting topological frequency-converting response**. The underlying principles of non-Hermitian topological amplification and singular-value topology are established foundations. Our contribution shows how those principles operate within one driven bosonic mode, where Floquet-induced topological interfaces select the input and output frequency windows and govern directional amplification.
